# Meta creative formats: which format to make next

A tier list (S to F) of about 51 Meta ad formats, meant as a prompt for "which format do I make next".

This is one practitioner's ranking (Dara Denney's), made for brands with creator budgets. It is not Meta's data and it is not a rule. A small account should start with formats it can make itself: a static image, a simple phone video, a carousel. Judge each one by the one stop rule in `paid-ads-strategy`, using your own results, not the tier letters.

Terms used below:
- UGC (user-generated content): ad-style content made in the look of an ordinary person's post, usually filmed on a phone by a creator you have agreed terms with.
- VSL (video sales letter): a longer video that explains a problem, then the solution, then makes an offer.
- Partnership ads: an ad that runs from a creator's own handle, with the creator's permission, so it shows their name and profile.
- DPA (dynamic product ads) or catalogue ads: ads that pull products automatically from a product catalogue you upload to Meta.
- Advantage+: Meta's automated campaign options, where Meta chooses more of the audience and placements.

## The one question behind the ranking

For any format, the author asks whether it is a scaler or a supporting format.

- A scaler reaches new, cold audiences and keeps working as spend rises. The author says these are rare.
- A supporting format mostly converts people who already know the brand. It is useful, but it will not open new audiences however much you spend.

A format is not bad for being supporting. It is a poor choice only if you expect it to reach cold audiences and it cannot. So aim for a mix: a few formats to reach new people, and a bench of others to convert them.

## Why the author ranks creator-fronted formats high

Practitioners believe Meta's delivery system now behaves more like it is matching ads to types of people than to interests alone. The author's view is that creator-fronted formats do well because they reach a type of person through a creator they already follow and trust. Meta has not confirmed this, so treat it as a working theory, not a fact. A partnership ad starts from the creator's own audience, which is one reason the author ranks founder content, partnership ads and authority ads highly.

A signal the author suggests watching: your month-on-month reach. If it keeps falling, your current audience may be worn out, and creator-fronted formats such as partnership ads are one way to reach new people.

## Production effort

- Low: copy plus one image; you can make it today (statics, a founder's letter).
- Medium: needs a creator, a shoot, a script or an edit.
- High: several people, usage rights, or heavy production.

## S tier: the author's top picks

| Format | Role | Effort | When to use it, and when to skip |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founder content | Reaches new people | Low to medium | The author calls it the reliable first winner. Tell why you built the brand; people with the same problem recognise themselves in it. Use it early, before you have proven creative. |
| Partnership ads | Reaches new people | Medium | A creator's post run as an ad from their handle, with their permission. It starts from the creator's own audience. Use it when you can agree terms with a creator. Skip it if you cannot find or afford one. |
| VSL (video sales letter) | Reaches new people | Medium to high | Suits anything the buyer must understand before buying, such as health or a complicated product. Skip it for cheap, impulse buys. The script is the ad, so put your effort there. |

A tactic the author suggests: when you hire creators for partnership ads, ask each to film a few simple, low-fi still images too (the way they would post a Story for the brand). It costs little extra.

## A tier

Worth testing once you have a first format running.

| Format | Role | Effort | When to use it, and when to skip |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amateur investigation | Reaches new people | Medium | A creator looks into your product or niche, for example by visiting competitors. Useful where doubt is the barrier. |
| Yapper ads | Reaches new people | Medium | A creator talks to camera in a personal story. High ceiling, hard to get right: it needs the right creator, script and setting. Skip it if you cannot cast well. |
| David and Goliath | Possibly reaches new people | Low to medium | The brand is the underdog against a bigger incumbent or obstacle. Use it only where there is a real, provable problem with the alternative. |
| Grid-style statics | Possibly reaches new people | Low | A grid of products, SKUs or bundles. Easy to make, so it is a quick one to test this week. |
| Authority ads | Reaches new people | Medium | A real expert (a doctor, say) fronts the ad. Suits trust-heavy categories. Claims must be true and provable. |
| Green-screen commentary | Possibly reaches new people | Medium | A creator cut out over other images or footage, commenting on it. The author recommends it for apparel, with an educational angle. |
| Catalogue ads (DPA) | Possibly reaches new people | Low to medium | Automated product ads from your catalogue. Often used only for retargeting, but the author says they can also reach people who have not visited your site. Use them if you have a real, up-to-date catalogue. |

## B tier: supporting formats

The author says these mostly convert people who already know the brand and are not expected to open cold audiences. Most are low effort (statics) unless noted.

TikTok love letter, a real short video (medium), callout ads, before and after (watch the claims), progression, tweet or Reddit style statics (often strong as the first frame), headline ads (need very good messaging), us-versus-them, lifestyle mirror-selfie or flat-lay stories, creator low-fi statics (the partnership tip above), objection-handling, founder's letter static, conversation ads (medium, hard to do well), educational infographics, mood board (apparel), comment-reply, challenging-your-beliefs (medium, needs B-roll, which is supporting footage, and a clear view of what your customer believes), and ugly or handwritten post-it style statics.

---

## C tier: situational or complicated

These win only in narrow cases and cost more effort than they return for most accounts.

AI animation (high effort; the author says it reaches new people at first but rarely holds), statistics ads (luxury or retail, awareness or traffic goals), celebrity (high effort; can work or waste money), AI avatar (the author says it has scaled with legal disclaimers but is fading as brands choose real creators), warehouse (high effort; suits sales), street interview, duet or reaction (needs the original creator's permission), ASMR (pet or beauty; needs a creator who makes ASMR), and ordinary UGC (still works, but the author sees general fatigue with staged problem-and-solution videos).

For street interviews and duets, the kit's rule is real people only, with their consent, and no staged "customers". See the notes in [short-form-video-specs.md](short-form-video-specs.md).

---

## D tier: rarely moves the needle

Breaking-news ads, AI billboard ads (only lands with punchy, taboo wording in supplements, per the author), and "get ready with me" or day-in-my-life videos (made for organic posting; the author says they rarely scale on paid unless the product fits a morning routine).

---

## E tier: mostly skip

Text-only ads (usually dull copy; the author's exception is a founder's letter during a sale), testimonial statics (only worth it with a truly outstanding real testimonial), listicles (the author says they worked a year or two ago and have faded), and carousels (the author finds the payoff not worth the effort; a small account may still choose to test one because it is cheap to make).

---

## F tier: the author says don't bother

- Press ads. Using a publication's name or logo without permission is a rights problem. Do not use a press logo you are not entitled to use.
- Podcast ads. The author says they are a waste unless a founder appears on a well-known show.
- Notes-app or fake-interface ads. The author says they do not convert. The kit's advice is to skip them, and to go further: ads that imitate phone system screens, alerts or chats can break Meta's ad rules and mislead people, so the kit does not teach them. Using an old app or phone screen as an obvious decorative frame is a visual style, not an imitation of a real alert; see Visual Styles in [static-ad-templates.md](static-ad-templates.md).

---

## Points to take from this list

- Treat the list as a mix of ideas, not a silver bullet. Founder content, partnership ads, authority, investigation, VSL and grid statics are the ones the author says reach new people. Everything else is for converting.
- Staged or recreated real-world scenes can feel fake. If a format needs real people, use real people, with consent. Never stage customers, reviews or results.
- The author sees fatigue with over-used formats. A fresh, honest format can beat a worn-out "best practice", but always check it against your own results.
