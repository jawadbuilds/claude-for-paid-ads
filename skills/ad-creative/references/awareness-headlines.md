# Awareness-based headline creative

Use this for text-led static concepts, especially B2B feed ads (ads from one business to another). These are compact treatments within the existing angle and template workflow. They do not replace audience research, the hook system or the platform specs.

## Choose the reader's next step

"Awareness stage" means how much the reader already knows. There are four stages, from least to most informed:

- **Unaware:** the reader does not yet think of their situation as a problem you could solve, or does not know your kind of product exists.
- **Problem-aware:** the reader knows they have a problem but has not looked for a fix.
- **Solution-aware:** the reader knows fixes exist and is weighing different approaches.
- **Product-aware:** the reader knows your product and needs a reason to choose it.

| Awareness | What the reader needs | Useful treatments | CTA direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unaware, or unfamiliar with the category | Recognise a specific lived situation | Feeling, conversation | Learn about the situation or how it works |
| Problem-aware | See a credible way out of a named friction | Pain and outcome, time-anchored friction | See the workflow or the explanation |
| Solution-aware | Understand a concrete way this approach differs | State change, documented statistic | Compare how it works, or watch a demo |
| Product-aware | Settle an objection with specific proof | Statistic, pain and outcome | Inspect the proof, try it, or book the relevant next step |

(CTA means call to action: the button or line that tells the reader what to do next.)

Stage is a working guess about this audience, not a diagnosis based on the platform alone. If it is unknown, state the assumption and propose two distinct stage hypotheses. A formula alone cannot prove an ad will convert.

## Six treatments

The examples below are invented illustrations of structure. They are not approved product claims, real customer quotes or ready-to-run ads. Replace their facts with the user's verified corpus, and fill every bracketed placeholder with a real, checked number or leave the treatment out. Each treatment can serve more than one stage, so choose based on what the reader already knows. (SaaS means software sold by subscription. DTC means direct to consumer: a brand selling straight to its customers.)

| Treatment | Construction and guardrail | Illustrative SaaS example | Illustrative DTC example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Feeling** | Name a recognisable feeling tied to a specific work or life moment. Avoid vague uplift or a forced positive-then-negative reveal. | "Leave Friday with the forecast finished." | "Dinner sorted before the commute home." |
| **Conversation** | Two short lines from a recognisable situation. Use an actual permitted exchange, or label invented dialogue as a dramatisation. Never fabricate a testimonial attribution. | "Is this the final sheet?" / "There are three final sheets." | "What's for dinner?" / "I'm still on the train." |
| **State change** | Show the current and the proposed workflow with concrete artefacts. State what changes, and do not force "not X, Y" copy or unsupported competitor claims. | "Bring the three approval threads into one queue." | "Pack tomorrow's lunch while tonight's dinner cooks." |
| **Time-anchored friction** | Name an outdated or repetitive process and the actual moment it bites. Criticise the process without shaming the buyer or inventing a trend. | "Month-end still starts with copying CSVs." | "The Sunday grocery list starts over again." |
| **Documented statistic** | One supported number plus the population, period or meaningful qualifier. Keep its real precision; a rounded number is not evidence of fakery. | "Pilot teams cut weekly reconciliation from [hours before] hours to [hours after]." | "[number] dinners packed in one delivery." |
| **Pain and outcome** | One named friction and a concrete next action or outcome, usually under ten words. Brevity is a drafting aim, not permission to drop a material qualifier. | "Find the missing invoice before close." | "Get dinner ready between calls." |

Read each draft against the AI-tells guidance in SKILL.md (and run it through `human-copy-check`). Do not produce six near-identical sentences just to fill a formula menu. Keep customer language where it is permitted and relevant. Facts can ground a paraphrase without becoming a fabricated quote.

## Gather proof before copy

Build a small evidence sheet with these columns: source ID, verbatim phrase or product fact, audience, date, allowed usage and limitations. Aim for five useful customer phrases when available, from more than one perspective. If the available corpus is smaller, report the gap and make smaller claims about variety. Do not invent quotes to reach a count. The same grounding rules apply to Path C batches in the skill.

Separate **language evidence** ("the export takes forever") from **claim evidence** (a measured reduction across a defined pilot). A complaint supports the pain angle, but it does not back a numerical performance promise. Keep a pilot's sample size and period in adjacent readable copy, or withhold the statistic when a truthful presentation cannot fit.

## Make the headline the visual

Apply this treatment to Headline Statement or Stat Callout from the static library when the words carry the concept. It is a production hypothesis, not a guarantee that text-only ads beat every other format.

- Start with one dominant sentence in a bold, legible sans-serif face. As a design starting point, let the headline fill roughly 50 to 70% of the composition, and adjust after testing at actual size.
- Use deliberate line breaks that keep phrases together. Keep the product name or logo secondary unless recognising the product is the concept's job.
- Test a dark background with light text, or a strong brand-colour background with contrasting text. Choose from the user's actual palette and check that it reads clearly. Do not prescribe a colour based on an unverified feed theme.
- Include the audience's specific moment, one defensible idea and enough qualification to read truthfully. Leave out stock imagery that competes with the sentence, tiny disclaimers, fake review screens, extra badges and an unsupported second claim.
- Make a preview at the **actual mobile feed display size**, not only on a desktop canvas. Check that a reviewer can read the whole headline and its material qualifiers without zooming. If not, shorten the claim or change the layout. Never solve it by shrinking required qualifications.
- Get current export dimensions, cropping, safe areas and text-field limits from the platform-spec reference and the selected placement. The 50 to 70% layout suggestion is not a platform rule or a text-coverage approval threshold.

## Deliver and learn

For each concept, provide the audience and stage assumption, a distinct angle, the treatment and static template, the headline, the supporting qualification, the CTA, the source IDs, and a visual specification with its mobile-readability check. Mark evidence gaps, and keep output as concepts for review unless publication is already authorised.

Test stage and angle hypotheses before fine font changes. Compare the relevant qualified outcome after the click with the existing control, keeping offer and audience stable where possible. Judge a headline on results only by the one stop rule in `paid-ads-strategy`. Strong click-through with weak qualified conversion is a reason to examine the promise and the landing page's continuity (see `landing-page-audit`), not proof of a winning headline.
