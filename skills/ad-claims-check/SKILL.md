# Ad claims check

Ad copy written quickly, by a person or an AI, tends to invent details that sound good. One false claim can get an ad rejected, an account restricted, or a customer misled. Check every ad against this list before it goes out.

## How to run it

1. List every factual claim in the ad: each number, price, date, promise and name. Include text on images, video overlays and carousel cards as well as the main text.
2. For each claim, ask the business owner for the source. Mark it **confirmed** (the owner showed where it comes from), **changed** (reworded to what the source supports) or **removed** (no source, so it comes out).
3. Rewrite anything that fails, and show the before and after.

## The ten checks

| Check | Fails when | Safer wording |
|---|---|---|
| **Numbers** | A count, percentage or statistic nobody can point to a source for | Remove it, or use the real figure with its source |
| **Scarcity** | "Only 12 places left" when the real number is different or unknown, or when there is no real limit | "Limited places", with no number, if a limit exists. If there is none, the line comes out |
| **Price** | A starting price is shown as the full price, or extras are hidden | "From [price]" or "starting at [price]" |
| **All-inclusive** | "Everything included" when some things cost extra | List what is included, or drop the phrase |
| **Deadlines and discounts** | A date or amount that is not confirmed in writing | "Closing soon" or "early-bird discount", with no figure until one is confirmed |
| **Borrowed names** | Wording that makes the business sound like part of a bigger, better-known organisation | Say where you are or what you do: "a design course in [city]", not "[Famous University] Design Course" |
| **Results and guarantees** | A promised outcome the business cannot guarantee | Describe what is provided, not what will happen |
| **Superlatives** | "Best", "number one", "the leading" with no independent proof | Say what is true and specific, or name the award and its year |
| **Comparisons** | A named or implied competitor, with no evidence for the comparison | Describe your own offer. Compare only with evidence the owner can show |
| **Testimonials** | A quote that was not said, was said without permission, or is not typical | Use real words from a real customer who agreed, and do not present a rare result as normal |

## Also check the platform's own rules

- Personal attributes: do not imply you know something personal about the reader ("Are you in debt?").
- Special categories: housing, jobs, credit, health and politics have extra limits on targeting and wording.
- Before and after images, and health or income claims, are often rejected.

If you are unsure whether a rule applies, say so and point the person to the platform's advertising policy page. Do not state a policy from memory as fact.

## Output

```
Claim                        Status      Fix
"Only 12 seats left"         changed     "Limited seats"
"From $49 a month"           confirmed   -
"Everything included"        removed     setup is charged separately

Result: ready to publish / fix [n] items first
```

## Rules

- You check wording. You do not give legal advice, and you say so if asked.
- Never invent a replacement fact. If the real number is unknown, the claim comes out.
